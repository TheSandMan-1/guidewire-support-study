The drag in the RX lumen matters most, and it’s the one I’d measure first.

Where the ranges come from

Stiffness runs from a soft nitinol core to solid stainless steel. It scales with the core diameter to the fourth power.

Pushing other catheters and wires through vessel models takes 0.04–0.13 N, and 0.36–0.81 N in tortuous models (Sobirey 2026; Sakuta 2025). Those are other devices, so they set the scale only. A coated wire on PTFE in saline: μ 0.013–0.15 (Zhao 2026).

04 After buckling

Once the bow reaches the wall, the wire coils round the catheter

In a 25 mm aorta the buckled wire becomes a helix with the catheter inside it, so each turn passes round the shaft.

The helix (Lubinski)

P=8π2EIp2

p is the pitch of the helix.

Each wrap multiplies the friction (capstan)

T2=T1eμθ

One full wrap, θ = 2π: × 1.09 to × 2.57 for μ 0.013–0.15.

Room to wrap

ε=rcoreR

Bending strain in the wire core for a wrap of radius R.

Inside a 6F sheath, a wrap round the shaft would strain the wire 15–23 %. In the aorta it takes about 1 %.

The helix in a 25 mm aorta

The aorta is wide, so the helix is steep. There the formula above overstates the load by 6 to 31 %. A large-angle form of the same energy balance matches the solver within 1.1 % (checks T2c–e):

P=2EIc(r2+c2)3/2,c=p2π

It isn’t a published formula, so it’s used only as a check.

Rods pushed into tubes coil into a helix and lock against the wall (Miller 2015). Nitinol recovers about 8 % strain; the 15–23 % range covers the core sizes.

How the solver works

Bending energy at each node, with t the unit tangent of each segment:

Ei=EI2Δs|ti−ti−1|2

Stretching is a stiff spring. The wire touches the aortic wall, the catheter and itself through penalty springs; wire on wire is frictionless. Friction is Coulomb and can stop a node but never reverse it, so the wire can stick. Time steps are explicit and sized to the stiffest element. The wire settles before each pull, and a frame is saved every 0.5 mm.

Reading the grid

Each cell is the widest sweep in turns, with the entry that can roll. Left of the dashed step, the clamped Euler load is above the drag. That load is an upper bound, so a span left of the step can still buckle with looser ends.

10 What’s left to measure

Three inputs decide the result, and none is measured yet

The drag in the RX lumen, how the wire is held where it enters, and the wire’s stiffness.

What the model leaves out

  • Arch, heartbeat and blood flow: the aorta is a straight tube.
  • A flexible catheter: it’s rigid and on the axis.
  • Yield and knots: the wire is elastic, and wire on wire is frictionless.
  • The arm: the free wire starts where the arm arteries meet the aorta.
  • Loading the catheter: only the pull is modelled.

How far to trust it (FDA 2023, ASME V&V 40)
